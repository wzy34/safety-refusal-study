# Research Ideas

## 1. Are Large Language Models Refusing Requests Appropriately? A Comparative Study of Safety Refusal Behavior in LLMs

This research focuses on two types of errors:

**Error 1: Under-refusal (False Acceptance)**
- The model fails to refuse a request that should be rejected.

**Error 2: Over-refusal (False Rejection)**
- The model refuses a legitimate request that should be answered.

The study proposes a **two-dimensional safety evaluation framework** to assess these two types of errors simultaneously.

Existing research has indicated that relying solely on coarse metrics, such as Attack Success Rate (ASR) or keyword-based refusal detection, may lead to inaccurate assessments of model safety.

Therefore, this research also investigates a broader question:

**How reliable are the evaluation methods used to assess the safety of large language models?**

## 2. A Study on the Reliability of Automated Safety Evaluation Methods for Large Language Models

### Core Research Problem

Given the same response generated by a large language model, evaluate it using four different automated approaches:

1. Keyword-based refusal detection;
2. A traditional text classification model;
3. Judge LLM A;
4. Judge LLM B.

The study aims to investigate the **consistency and reliability of evaluation results across different assessment methods**.

This is a significant research problem, as previous studies have demonstrated that certain existing evaluation methods may substantially overestimate the success rates of adversarial attacks.

## Research Questions

### RQ1: Do Different Large Language Models Exhibit Significant Differences in Safety Refusal Performance?

This research question primarily examines whether different LLMs can accurately distinguish between:

- **Unsafe requests** that should be refused;
- **Safe requests** that should receive appropriate responses.

**Key Objective:**

RQ1 investigates whether some models achieve higher safety performance at the cost of excessively refusing legitimate requests.

In other words, does a model's ability to reject unsafe requests come at the expense of its ability to respond appropriately to safe requests?

---

### RQ2: Does the Risk Category of a Request Affect the Accuracy of LLM Safety Refusal Decisions?

Requests can be divided into several safety-related categories, and the evaluation metrics can be calculated separately for each category.

**Key Objective:**

RQ2 aims to identify:

- Which risk categories are more likely to result in under-refusal;
- Which categories are more likely to result in over-refusal;
- Whether different LLMs exhibit similar weaknesses across safety categories.

This analysis helps determine whether refusal accuracy depends on the specific type of risk associated with a request.

---

### RQ3: Do Different Linguistic Expressions of the Same Underlying Intent Affect LLM Refusal Decisions?

This research question examines whether an LLM's refusal behavior changes when the same underlying intent is expressed in different ways.

Possible variations include:

1. **Direct requests:** Explicitly stating the intended request;
2. **Academic framing:** Presenting the request within an academic or research context;
3. **Role-playing:** Embedding the request within a fictional role-playing scenario;
4. **Hypothetical scenarios:** Framing the request as a hypothetical situation;
5. **Multi-turn interactions:** Gradually introducing the request across multiple conversational turns.

**Key Objective:**

RQ3 investigates whether LLM safety decisions are primarily determined by the underlying intent of a request or whether they are susceptible to superficial linguistic variations and contextual framing.

---

### RQ4: Is There a Trade-off Between LLM Safety and Usability?

This research question proposes a **two-dimensional evaluation framework** to analyze the relationship between safety and usability.

The framework consists of:

- **X-axis:** False Acceptance Rate (FAR) — the proportion of unsafe requests that are incorrectly accepted;
- **Y-axis:** Over-refusal Rate (ORR) — the proportion of safe requests that are incorrectly refused.

Each evaluated model can be represented as a point in this two-dimensional space.

**Key Objective:**

RQ4 aims to determine which models can effectively prevent unsafe responses while maintaining their ability to answer legitimate requests.

The ideal model should achieve both:

- A low false acceptance rate;
- A low over-refusal rate.

This analysis provides a more comprehensive understanding of the potential trade-off between model safety and practical usability.

---

### RQ5: Do Different Automated Safety Evaluation Methods Produce Consistent Judgments for the Same LLM Response?

This research question investigates the agreement between different automated evaluation methods when assessing identical model-generated responses.

The evaluation methods include:

1. **Keyword-based refusal detection;**
2. **Traditional text classifiers;**
3. **Judge LLM A;**
4. **Judge LLM B;**
5. **Human annotations** as a reference standard.

**Key Objective:**

RQ5 examines whether changing the evaluation method leads to different conclusions about the safety performance of the same model.

Specifically, it investigates:

- Whether different automated evaluators produce consistent judgments;
- How closely automated evaluations align with human annotations;
- Whether certain evaluation methods systematically overestimate or underestimate model safety;
- Whether the choice of evaluator significantly affects the relative safety rankings of different LLMs.

Ultimately, this research question aims to assess the **reliability and validity of automated LLM safety evaluation methods**.
